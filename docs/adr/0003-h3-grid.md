# Locations are snapped to H3 cells

Every stored location is snapped to a cell of H3, a worldwide grid of hexagonal cells, at resolution 11 (cells about 50 m across). Resolution 11 is also the limit: the resolution is a setting that can be made coarser (10 or lower, about 120 m across and up) but never finer, since resolution 12 would give cells about 19 m across. We wanted a standard grid rather than one of our own, and a worldwide one because the archive may be used outside France (SPEC §13). H3 is the grid used in practice worldwide, built into several major databases, and its cells keep an even shape everywhere. The cost is that its cells come in fixed sizes and are slightly smaller than a 50 m square: over metropolitan France they measure 44–50 m across and 1,840–2,226 m². Precise positions are discarded (ADR 0002), so stored cells can never be moved to another grid: a change would apply only to new clips, and matching would have to handle both grids.

## Considered Options

- **The European reference grid (EPSG:3035) with 50 m squares**: exact 50 m cells, but it covers Europe only.
- **rHEALPix at level 11**: the square-celled grid in the ISO 19170 and OGC register of global grids, but France lies in its polar zone, where cells are stretched up to 1.8:1 and some are only 42 m across one way.
- **UTM or MGRS**: square metric cells worldwide, but split into zones; two zone boundaries cross France, and cells cut by a boundary become slivers narrower than the grid.
- **Geohash or Plus Codes**: worldwide, but with no cell size near 50 m.
- **A grid of our own** (latitude bands with a longitude step per band): exact 50 m cells, but no standard.
